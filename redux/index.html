The Future From Memory

Emma Kay, made with e-2

Emma Kay’s work reconstructs epic histories, geographies and fictions entirely from memory. The Future From Memory turns the method forwards: a history of the world still to come, from the twenty-first century to the thirty-second and beyond, told in 269 paragraphs.

It was made in Macromedia Flash in five segments, each loading the next, and distributed on CD in 2003 to run full-screen from a projector program on Mac and Windows. Flash Player is no longer supported anywhere, so in 2026 the original files were played back in Ruffle, an open-source Flash emulator, and rendered to video frame by frame.

Every video is here twice. The original piece had an aspect ratio of 4:3; the widescreen 16:9 version here removes the top quarter of the picture, which is blank in every frame, so no text is lost.
